# Leveraging AI in M&A: Lessons from GCs on the Frontlines

Most articles on AI in M&A read like they were written by a marketing team.

“AI will transform your legal department!”

But what does that actually mean for in-house lawyers, especially General Counsel leading M&A deals?

I had a conversation with [Bill Price](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bill-price/) a New York-based litigator turned GC who has spent the last two decades in the thick of M&A, from helping a SaaS firm IPO, to being the lead legal support for a $40 billion enterprise sales division at [AT&T](https://www.linkedin.com/company/att/) , to turning around and selling companies...

Let’s be honest, most of the content out there about AI and M&A is high-level fluff.

“AI will transform the deal process”… sure, but how?

And when it comes to AI and M&A, he’s got some *very real lessons* .

This isn’t theory. It’s lived experience.

#### 1. Being a tech GC instead of Being a GC in M&A.

The mantra? And one I'm putting in my back pocket for the future!

“Keep us out of jail and get things done.”

That’s it. That’s the role.

And it hit me, so many junior GCs get stuck in lawyer mode. They’re comfortable redlining contracts, negotiating clauses, fixing the messes. But what this GC kept saying is: *that’s table stakes* .

The real job is *driving the business forward* . He estimates his role is 95% business person, 5% legal operator.

**Here’s what that looks like in practice:**

Thinking strategically about the balance sheet.Anticipating problems before they blow up.Asking: how do we accelerate revenue, smooth bottlenecks, and position the company for a better exit?

That’s where AI starts to make sense, not as a shiny object, but as a tool to shift lawyers from reactive problem-solvers to proactive business leaders.

The real job is driving the business forward and growing the top line…

---

#### 2. Start Small, Prove Value

I know some of you are thinking: “But I don’t have budget for AI tools. The board isn’t going to approve some six-figure experiment.”

Good news: you don’t need it.

The smartest adoption I’ve seen is about picking *one small, high-impact problem* and solving it.

The GC gave this example: sales kept hitting the same contract red lines quarter after quarter. It was slowing revenue and frustrating everyone. By analyzing those patterns with AI, legal was able to rework the playbook, clear the bottleneck, and speed up deal closure.

That’s how you win buy-in. Start with the crawl stage. Solve something measurable. Prove value. Then expand.

---

#### 3. Crawl → Walk → Run in M&A

When you’re in the middle of a transaction, one of the first questions the GC asks the banker is: *“Which Virtual Data Room (VDR) are we using?”*

*Spoiler alert its not the only question you should be asking....*

That’s because due diligence lives and dies on how you manage documents.

This is where AI already shines:

Aggregating and structuring huge volumes of contracts.Summarising key clauses across multiple acquisitions.Flagging risk before it becomes a negotiation nightmare.

But don’t try to automate everything from day one. Crawl (targeted use cases). Walk (expand into playbook updates, dashboards, deal trackers). Then run (integrate AI across the full lifecycle of M&A).

---

#### 4. Optics and Influence Matter

One of my favourite stories from this GC was about optics.

They literally redesigned contracts to look better. Cleaner formatting, color logo, clearer visuals. Make the contract a differentiated product to sell.

Why? Because when a CEO or board member picks up a 60-page SPA, they don’t want to wade through walls of text. The legal substance didn’t change, but the perception of value did.

That’s the lesson for AI too. Don’t just *use* it, *communicate its value* . Show how it accelerates revenue. Show how it makes negotiations smoother. Show how it protects the business.

---

#### 5. Imposter Syndrome Is Real. But Flipping It Creates Influence

Many GCs feel like imposters when they first get pulled into board-level M&A discussions. You go from being the lawyer in the room to being expected to act like a business partner overnight.

Here’s the advice:

Embrace that shift. You *are* a business partner now.Let AI do some of the heavy lifting on the legal grunt work.Use the insights to drive higher-level conversations: *“Here’s where we keep losing time. Here’s the fix. Here’s the ROI.”*

That’s how you move from support function to strategic advisor.

---

#### 6. Don’t Chase Shiny Objects

Not all AI tools are worth it. This GC was clear: pick two or three with *real use cases* .

If e-discovery is your pain point, pick decoverAI; if it's M&A due diligence, pick Emma or from his experience with tech.

If your sales team is screaming about bottlenecks, start with analyzing approval authority processes and have AI build a streamlined playbook-publish it in Slack and build a culture of empowerment and autonomy

The trap is adopting tools because they look impressive, not because they solve your pain.

---

#### AI Won’t Replace GCs (except those who don't use it). It Will Make Them

It Will Make Them Indispensable

Here’s the truth: AI won’t make the GC redundant. It will make them more central and provide more time to be strategic to the business than ever before

But only if you approach it the right way:

Start small.Focus on business impact.Measure and communicate wins.Grow into bigger use cases.

M&A is one of the most complex, high-stakes things a GC will ever do. AI won’t take away that responsibility. But it can give you leverage, the kind of leverage that lets you move from being the lawyer cleaning up the mess… to the business leader who prevents it in the first place.
