# Legal Ops Before AI: Why You Can’t Run Before You Can Walk

If you follow me here you'll know I talk a lot about AI in legal, the risk, the reward, the future. Many rambles of what I've learnt over 20 years advising and working with clients on all things tech.

But here’s the truth: without strong legal operations, AI will never deliver on its promise. It’s like trying to run before you can walk, or building a skyscraper without laying the foundations. Legal Ops isn't a cost centre, its the command centre and THE backbone to connecting legal with the wider business. Think a handshake that helps things run smoothly.

I once heard a client describe it perfectly, as the “below the table juggling balls.”

She said: imagine the balls above the table, the client discussions, the M&A deals, the litigation matters, and everything else in plain sight.

Legal ops is like the command room under the table, the part you don’t always see, but without it, the whole act falls apart. That’s where the broken links happen if it’s not running well.

Legal ops is that foundation. It’s the processes, systems, and culture that make technology work, whether that’s contract automation, AI-assisted review, or analytics dashboards. Without it, even the most advanced tools risk becoming expensive shelfware.

Many of my clients ask me to explain the what the why and the how so I hope this helps :)

#### What is Legal Operations?

Legal Operations, often called Legal Ops, is the business side of running a legal department or team. It involves managing people, processes, technology, and data to improve efficiency and reduce risk.

Think of Legal Ops as the engine room that helps lawyers focus on practicing law by handling the day-to-day operational details. It covers areas like budgeting, vendor management, technology adoption, and process improvement.

In today’s fast-paced legal environment, Legal Ops is key to transforming traditional legal teams into strategic business partners.

---

#### Why Legal Operations Matters

Legal departments face growing pressure to do more with less, delivering legal services faster, smarter, and more cost-effectively.

Legal Ops helps by:

Streamlining workflows and reducing repetitive tasksManaging and evaluating technology toolsEnhancing data-driven decision-makingImproving collaboration between legal and business teamsEnsuring compliance and risk management

Without Legal Ops, legal teams risk inefficiency, higher costs, and missed opportunities to add strategic value.

---

#### Key Roles and Responsibilities in Legal Ops

Legal Ops professionals act as a bridge between lawyers, IT, finance, and business stakeholders to keep the legal function running smoothly and aligned with company goals.

---

#### The Benefits of Legal Operations

By investing in Legal Operations, organisations can work more efficiently, control costs, strengthen risk management, and ensure compliance. All whilst freeing legal teams to focus on high-value work. It also opens the door to innovation, using technology and data more effectively. Legal ops is no longer a “nice to have”; it’s essential for any legal team that wants to thrive in today’s competitive landscape.

### Building your Legal Ops Function

#### Establishing a Legal Ops Team

Starting or refining a Legal Ops function begins with setting clear goals aligned to your organisation’s priorities, defining what success looks like, and securing leadership buy-in. From there, focus on assessing your legal team’s pain points, setting objectives like improving efficiency or managing costs, building a team with the right blend of operational, technical, and legal expertise, and creating strong communication channels between Legal Ops, lawyers, and the wider business.

It’s can also be worth bringing in external expertise where needed, someone who has seen both the wins and the failures with other clients, can ensure you’re building from the ground up in the right direction, and can act as a neutral intermediary when engaging with legal teams or the wider business to help sell the vision internally.

Remember culture over tech and trust wins adoption rates internally...

---

#### Defining Objectives and KPIs

Clear objectives guide Legal Ops efforts and make success measurable. Whether the aim is reducing contract turnaround times, cutting legal spend, or increasing technology adoption. Setting KPIs early, such as contracts processed per month, time to complete due diligence, percentage of spend under management, or user adoption rates for legal tech, creates accountability and helps justify ongoing investment.

One size rarely fits all. It’s imperative to understand your own processes, people, and workflows before you even start looking at tools, tech, or AI. Think of it like a plaster, do this the wrong way round and it won’t stick for long; do it the right way, and it sets you up for success.

---

#### Process Optimisation and Continuous Improvement

Legal ops isn’t about fixing something and moving on. I can see you all thinking of a tick box spreadsheet already and that magic complete button 👀

It’s a long-term commitment that works best when you’re willing to tweak and adapt. Often, you don’t truly know what a process needs until you start working with it. Mapping workflows helps spot bottlenecks and inefficiencies, and tools like process maps or case management systems can make tasks easier to visualise and streamline. The best results come from continuous improvement, regularly reviewing processes, gathering feedback, and adjusting tools and workflows as needs evolve. Teams and people change and processes and tech is no different!

---

### Using Case Mapping and Process Visualisation

#### What is Case Mapping?

Case mapping breaks complex legal matters into clear, manageable steps. From initial intake to final resolution, creating a detailed roadmap of the process.

In my experience, taking the time to map a case or transaction often reveals insights you wouldn’t spot otherwise. It helps teams clarify the scope of work, identify dependencies and bottlenecks, allocate resources effectively, improve coordination, and spot opportunities for automation or process improvement. By visualising the entire workflow, case mapping turns complex, often chaotic matters into structured, transparent plans that are much easier to manage and optimise.

Spoiler alert you can't do this without speaking to your people. You need to work with teams, find out who is involved in processes to map these out effectively. Yes I can hear you telling me that takes too long but trust me its worth the investment.

You're also setting up that all important TRUST element with your teams when you do roll any changes out as the right people would have been involved so it works. I'm not saying involve every member of every team, thats not logical but the rest of the team will feel heard if the right one was involved.

---

#### Identifying Bottlenecks and Opportunities

Clear, visual overviews of every step in a legal process, makes it easier to see where delays or inefficiencies occur.

In my experience, they often highlight things like redundant approvals, repetitive manual data entry (I see you teams with 4 systems that you enter the same information into 👀), or bottlenecks from unclear handoffs.

Once these areas are identified, Legal Ops can take targeted action, redesign workflows, reallocate resources, or introduce automation to handle repetitive tasks. Tackling these pain points proactively helps streamline processes, cut turnaround times, and improve the overall quality of legal work. Remember its not always about working faster, but working better.

#### Real-Life Examples

I'm going to give you a lifeline here. 20 years of working with lawyers has shown me the use case list is endless, across every function but here are some examples to set the scene better.

**Reducing Contract Review Times**

A client faced delays in their contract review process due to unclear workflows and multiple handoffs between legal, procurement, and business teams. By mapping out the entire contract lifecycle, from request to final signature, they identified redundant review steps and bottlenecks caused by inconsistent approval protocols.

We redesigned the workflow to streamline approvals and introduced automated reminders for pending tasks. As a result, the contract review cycle time was reduced, enabling faster deal closures and improved stakeholder satisfaction.

Note how I didn't even mention AI here 👀

Its not always AI thats needed sometimes it's just straight forward automation. We're quick to want those shiny new things without really thinking is it for me. Don't get me wrong I had a similar client to the above where they also were wasting time on trying to match previous precedents during the review phase. This is where AI comes in and can help with the redlining phase. I also had another where the M&A team simply needed to review risky clauses quickly. Enter the tool which allowed us to pre define those clauses and then analyse 20,000 contracts (yes imagine going through those manually...) extracting said clauses and providing a nice XLS report for the lawyers to do the lawyering. I mean as nice as an XLS report can ever be...

### Technology in Legal Operations

Now lets get into what you know is my favourite subject. Legal Tech. I talk a lot about not running before you can walk which I stand by but tech is also the magic that glues all that prep work together.

---

#### Legal Tech Categories

Legal tech covers a broad range of tools designed to improve different parts of legal work. Understanding these categories helps you focus on solutions that truly meet your team’s needs. Remember rarely does one size fits all work. It has to fit THE use case and THE people. An example of some different types for you:

**Matter Management:** Software that centralises all case-related information, documents, deadlines, and communications. It helps legal teams organise workloads, track progress, and improve collaboration across departments.**Contract Lifecycle Management (CLM):** Tools that automate and streamline the entire contract process, from drafting and negotiation to approval, storage, and renewal reminders. CLMs reduce errors, speed up cycle times, and improve compliance. These have had a face lift since the boom of AI as now many can do this and more. AI brings in things like being able to auto redline and speed up review parsing a clause in a new contract to those in your fallback positions. **E-discovery and Document Review:** Platforms that support efficient collection, processing, and review of large volumes of documents for litigation or investigations. Many include features like AI-assisted tagging, deduplication, and predictive coding to reduce manual review effort.**AI-Powered Assistants and Analytics:** Advanced AI tools that can answer questions about documents, summarise content, flag risks, and provide insights based on legal data. They help teams make faster, more informed decisions and identify hidden risks early.**Legal Research Tools:** Databases and AI-enhanced platforms that help lawyers quickly find relevant cases, statutes, and legal precedents, saving hours of manual research.**Compliance and Risk Management Software:** Tools that monitor regulatory changes, automate compliance workflows, and help manage risk across jurisdictions.**Billing and Time Tracking:** Applications that simplify tracking legal time and expenses, automate billing processes, and integrate with finance systems for improved accuracy.**Collaboration Platforms:** Secure communication and document-sharing tools designed for legal teams and external counsel to work together seamlessly and maintain confidentiality.

---

#### Evaluating Tech

When choosing technology, it’s important to think beyond the shiny demo features. Ask yourself: does it solve your specific pain points? Is it intuitive for your team to use? Can it integrate with existing systems? Does it meet legal and data privacy requirements? And finally, what level of vendor support and roadmap can you rely on?
